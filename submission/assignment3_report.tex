\sectiontitle{1\quad Problem statement and objective}
Develop and compare four machine-translation systems for English-to-French parallel text:
a basic encoder--decoder Transformer, mT5-small, a LLaMA-family decoder-only model, and
Gemma-2-2B-it. All systems must translate the same held-out test set. Evaluation covers
BLEU, chrF, training or fine-tuning time where applicable, inference time, parameter count,
qualitative translation quality, sampling temperature, and responsible-AI risks.

\sectiontitle{2\quad Dataset description and source}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Dataset:} OPUS Books, English--French parallel corpus.
\item \textbf{Sources:} \href{https://opus.nlpl.eu/legacy/opus-books.php}{OPUS Books}
and \href{https://huggingface.co/datasets/Helsinki-NLP/opus_books}{Hugging Face dataset card}.
\item \textbf{Selection:} public book-domain parallel text; shuffled with seed 4403.
\item \textbf{Subset:} 10,000 training pairs, 1,000 validation pairs, and 1,000 test pairs.
\item \textbf{Cleaning:} whitespace normalization, removal of empty pairs, and filtering
of source/target sentences longer than 250 characters.
\item \textbf{Preprocessing:} word tokenization and learned vocabularies for the basic model;
SentencePiece tokenization for mT5; model tokenizers and translation prompts for decoder-only
models. The test sentences and references are shared across all four systems.
\end{itemize}

\sectiontitle{5\quad Model comparison}
The basic Transformer is transparent and task-specific but must learn translation from the
selected subset without pretraining. mT5 has a direct sequence-to-sequence inductive bias and
multilingual pretraining, so it is expected to provide the strongest quality per parameter
among the fine-tuned systems. Decoder-only models can use instruction following and LoRA,
but their prompts increase input length and their larger parameter counts increase memory and
inference cost. The final best-performing model must be selected from the generated BLEU and
chrF results rather than from model size or expectation.

\sectiontitle{6\quad Sampling-temperature analysis}
The default comparison uses deterministic generation for reproducibility. A separate analysis
should generate identical probe sentences at $T\in\{0.2,0.7,1.0\}$:
\[
P(w_i\mid w_{<i})\propto\exp(z_i/T).
\]
Low temperature produces stable, conservative translations but may repeat or omit content.
Higher temperature increases lexical variation but can introduce grammatical errors, omissions,
and fluent hallucinations. For production translation, deterministic decoding or a validated
low temperature is preferable; temperature should not be used to hide low-confidence outputs.

\sectiontitle{7\quad Responsible and ethical AI}
Translation systems may reproduce gender, regional, occupational, or dialect bias in training
data. They can hallucinate fluent details, omit negation or numbers, and misrepresent idioms,
honorifics, food, place names, and culturally specific references. Medical, legal, financial,
identity, and personal text requires special caution because a plausible mistranslation can cause
real harm.

Mitigations include representative and licensed data, language-pair and subgroup audits,
named-entity and numeric-preservation checks, domain terminology constraints, human review for
high-risk content, uncertainty escalation, privacy-preserving processing, and disclosure that
outputs are machine-generated. BLEU and chrF measure overlap, not safety or cultural adequacy;
qualitative review of saved samples is therefore required.

\sectiontitle{8\quad Conclusion}
This work provides a reproducible comparison of encoder--decoder and decoder-only translation
architectures on a shared English--French test set. The from-scratch Transformer establishes a
transparent baseline, mT5 tests multilingual sequence-to-sequence fine-tuning, and TinyLlama
and Gemma test parameter-efficient decoder-only adaptation. The final numerical conclusion
should be inserted from \texttt{results/metrics.csv} after execution; no unrun or fabricated
metric is reported here.
